# Journal positioning for the completed computational study

## Recommended first discussion with coauthors: *Radiation Research*

The Radiation Research Society describes *Radiation Research* as publishing original work on physical, chemical, and biological radiation effects, including dosimetry and chemical agents used to understand radiation effects ([official author information](https://www.radres.org/page/AuthorInfo)). That is the closest match to the paper's integrated transport–radiolysis–biological-plausibility scope. A regular research article should lead with the actual-edep method, event-level statistics, negative neural-enrichment result, and bounded chemical interpretation.

Likely reviewer expectations are accurate source/dosimetry provenance, validated scoring, defensible uncertainty, radiation-chemistry terminology, and biological relevance beyond a pure transport exercise. The current computation meets those methodological expectations. Specimen-plane spectrum/dose measurements and one LITE-1/redox perturbation experiment would most increase acceptance probability, but their absence can be presented as a bounded limitation rather than concealed.

## Strong alternative: *Journal of Radiation Research*

JRR explicitly covers fundamental radiation science spanning biology, chemistry, and physics, while cautioning that it does not accept pure physics or chemistry ([official scope](https://academic.oup.com/jrr/pages/About)). The work qualifies because it is organized around a biological observation and cross-tissue interpretation. Its Regular Paper format permits up to 7,000 words and requires Introduction, Materials and Methods, Results, and Discussion ([official instructions](https://academic.oup.com/jrr/pages/Instructions_For_Authors)).

The current manuscript structure is already close. Reviewers will likely ask why model validation substitutes for direct experimental validation; the response must emphasize Cannon's genetic/tissue-sufficiency data as the biological anchor and keep causal language out. The most valuable addition would again be specimen-plane dosimetry or a targeted scavenger experiment.

## Radiation-physics/chemistry alternative: *Radiation Physics and Chemistry*

The publisher describes RPC as covering radiation transport, dosimetry, radiation-induced radical reactions, kinetics, and computational radiation-chemistry models, with uncertainties and statistical analysis expected where relevant ([official Elsevier journal listing](https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/subjects/physical-sciences-and-engineering/physics)). This is an excellent methodological fit if the manuscript foregrounds actual-edep scoring, neural-volume convergence, Geant4-DNA yields, and chemical-rate bracketing.

The biological LITE-1 framing should be presented as the application rather than a claim of neuromodulatory mechanism. Reviewers may request stronger experimental spectra or track-chemistry validation. A measured at-sample spectrum would most improve this route.

## Neuroscience-continuity alternative: *Frontiers in Neuroscience*

The parent journal spans genes-to-behavior neuroscience and computational/technological approaches, but states that computational studies of public data require appropriate validation ([official scope](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/about)). Publication alongside the original Cannon study would provide continuity. The current negative neural-targeting result and ectopic-muscle interpretation are scientifically relevant, but the paper may be judged more radiation-physics than neuroscience. The Neural Technology section's scope emphasizes neuro/nanotechnology convergence and may be less direct ([section scope](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/sections/neural-technology/about)).

A biological coauthor experiment—especially tissue-specific redox perturbation during X-ray exposure—would most improve this option.

## Higher-bar biophysics option: *Biophysical Journal*

The Biophysical Society identifies *Biophysical Journal* as its flagship for biophysical research emphasizing excellence, integrity, and transparency ([journal page](https://www.biophysics.org/biophysical-journal)). The multiscale model is compatible in principle, but current work stops before a calibrated molecular/channel model. Reviewers would likely require direct experimental validation or a quantitatively constrained receptor/cellular transfer function. It is therefore a plausible later target only if new biological measurements close that gap.

## Positioning decision

Prepare the coauthor draft in a format compatible with *Radiation Research* or JRR. Decide between them after Dr. Bolding evaluates the experimental framing and publication constraints. The computation is sufficient for a bounded physical-plausibility paper; no journal choice converts Level-1 chemical opportunity into a molecular mechanism.
