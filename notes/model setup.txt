eah, exactly. I overcorrected into “follow Wu” when the real point is:

Wu is evidence for one valid Geant4-ready reduction, not a prescription for our biology.

They had a radiation-ecology dose-coefficient question. Their relevant targets were whole body, digestive system, reproductive system, and rest-of-body. That is why their model stops there. They were not trying to answer neuron-level ROS/radiomodulation. The paper explicitly says their adult model included the external contour plus digestive and reproductive internal surfaces, and their internal exposure model designates digestive/reproductive systems as source organs.

So for us, the correct decision rule is not:

What did Wu include?

It is:

Which anatomical structures materially affect transport, source terms, or scoring for our ROS/radiomodulation question?

That gives a better methodology:

The path I would actually commit to

1. Physical transport mesh: keep it mesh-based, not voxel-first

Use a cleaned mesh compartment model as the Geant4 physical geometry.

Why? Because Wu argues mesh-type models are relevant at the C. elegans micron scale and can be more realistic/efficient than voxel models for Monte Carlo, and their implementation was explicitly mesh/remesh-based.

So physical geometry should be a small-to-moderate set of non-overlapping mesh volumes.

2. Compartment selection should be based on our scientific question

For your ROS/neuron project, I would include more than Wu, but still not all 694 objects as material volumes.

Physical material/source/target compartments

Start with:

whole body / body envelope
digestive system: mouth/pharynx/intestine
reproductive system
body-wall muscle aggregate
hypodermis / cuticle shell, if we can make the shell clean
excretory system/canal, if clean enough
nervous-system aggregate, maybe
rest-of-body parent material

That is not blindly copying Wu. It extends Wu to compartments that matter for radiomodulation.

3. Neurons: two-tier handling, not “just ignore them”

This is the key nuance.

For transport material, individual neurons probably do not need to be separate physical volumes initially. Wu found water vs soft tissue changed results by only a few percent, and they even argue water is reasonable because it has better cross-section support and is useful for future Geant4-DNA-style modeling.

But for biological scoring, neurons absolutely matter. So:

individual neurons = scoring atlas
nervous system aggregate = optional physical mesh compartment

This means Geant4 can transport through:

whole body / muscle / pharynx / intestine / nervous-system aggregate

while the analysis still reports:

dose/ROS near AVAL
dose/ROS near AVAR
dose/ROS near PVDL/PVDR
dose/ROS near nerve ring

That is a better compromise than either extreme.

The specific answer to “what about structures they excluded?”

They excluded most structures because their endpoint did not need them.

For us:

digestive/reproductive:
    include because they are major compartments and useful literature match

muscle:
    include because radiomodulation/behavior and body-wall response matter

hypodermis/cuticle:
    include if we can model as shell/boundary; otherwise body envelope + scoring label

excretory/canal:
    include if clean and not too overlap-heavy; otherwise scoring/source label

neurons:
    include as scoring atlas immediately
    optionally include nervous-system aggregate as physical compartment
    only make selected individual neurons explicit physical volumes later if the physics demands it

Why not all neurons as physical volumes?

Not because it is impossible in principle. Because it likely adds little transport accuracy while adding huge geometry risk.

At X-ray/electron energies relevant to dose/ROS, if the neuron material is basically water/soft tissue, the particle transport difference between “neuron material” and “surrounding soft tissue” is small compared to the uncertainty in geometry, source, ROS chemistry, and scoring.

But neuron location is crucial. So preserve neuron location for scoring.

Final decision logic

Use the most detailed physical mesh model that passes these tests:

non-overlapping
watertight
visually anatomically sane
Geant4 navigates without warnings
does not require biologically destructive booleans

Everything else stays in the scoring atlas.
