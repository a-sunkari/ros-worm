# Final executive summary

ROS-Worm now contains a paper-ready computational physical-plausibility study of the Cannon/Bolding LITE-1 X-ray response. Two direct 100-million-history Geant4 runs reduce neural-dose Monte Carlo error to 3.6% focused and 7.1% diffuse. Actual energy-deposition steps are scored against both the original high-resolution nervous surface and an analysis-only exact union of 276 verified closed nervous objects.

The final neural/whole-worm dose ratios are `0.932±0.034` focused and `0.873±0.062` diffuse; muscle ratios are `1.060±0.009` and `1.083±0.018`. Neural and muscle tissues therefore receive broadly comparable, whole-worm-order physical dose. About 14.2–14.4% of whole-worm energy is deposited within 5 µm of the nervous surface, essentially matching muscle-surface proximity. Ninety-nine matched nervous-atlas controls per configuration do not justify a neuron-selective transport claim.

Six new Geant4-DNA chemistry runs use actual local deposited energy and local edep-weighted electron spectra. They predict prompt homogeneous-water OH, H2O2, hydrated-electron, H-radical, and H3O+ yields from picoseconds to one microsecond. Primary pulse-radiolysis kinetics support only broad Trp-like, thiol-like, and PRDX-like chemical interaction opportunities. No LITE-1 activation, channel gating, calcium, or behavioral probability is modeled.

The strongest supported interpretation is that Cannon/Bolding-like X-rays provide a rapid radiophysical/radiochemical stimulus throughout irradiated tissues, while LITE-1 expression may supply biological tissue specificity. This is consistent with ectopic muscle LITE-1 sensitivity and does not require neurons to be privileged radiation absorbers.

The repository includes reproducible tables, nine PNG/PDF figures, a journal-style manuscript, supplementary methods/tables, literature and uncertainty audits, a hostile Reviewer-2 report and response, journal positioning, and a fail-loud release audit. Overall status is **COAUTHOR REVIEW READY**. Remaining decisive gaps—specimen-plane dosimetry/spectra, animal-specific registration, intracellular scavenging, and LITE-1/redox causal tests—require experiments.
